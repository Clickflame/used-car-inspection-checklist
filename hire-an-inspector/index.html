When To Hire A Used Car Inspector

A checklist is a good first pass. Here is when to bring in someone who does this every day.

What A Professional Adds

  • A Trained Eye Underneath

    Leaks, frame damage, worn bushings and torn boots are hard to judge from your knees with a phone light. An inspector knows what normal looks like on that model.

  • A Real Scan Tool

    A professional scan reads stored and pending codes from more than the engine computer. It also shows whether the codes were cleared before you arrived.

  • Model Knowledge

    Every model has its known weak points. An inspector who has seen fifty of them knows where to look first.

  • A Written Report

    Photos and notes in writing give you something to negotiate with, or a clear reason to walk away.

When It Is Worth It

  • The car is in another city or state and you cannot see it yourself
  • It is sold as is, at auction, or by a seller you found online
  • The price is more than you could stand to lose
  • You found a red flag and still want the car
  • It is a truck, diesel, hybrid, EV or classic with costly parts

What It Costs

PPI Auto charges $225 for most used cars. That covers 122 points, an OBD-II scan and a road test, with the report sent the same day. See the PPI Auto price list for trucks, RVs and other vehicle types.

How To Set It Up

  1. Tell the seller you want the car inspected before you buy. A no is your answer.
  2. Book the inspection and give the address where the car sits.
  3. The inspector goes to the car. You do not need to be there.
  4. Read the report, then buy, negotiate or walk away.
