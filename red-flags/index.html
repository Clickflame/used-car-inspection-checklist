Ten Red Flags That Should End The Deal

Most used car problems can be priced in. These ten usually cannot.

By Anthony Limpert, mobile mechanic and shop owner. Author source EEAT Authors.

  1. The Seller Will Not Allow An Inspection

    A seller with nothing to hide lets a mechanic look. If the answer is no, thank them and leave. No other check on this page matters more.

  2. The Title Does Not Add Up

    The name on the title should match the seller ID. A lien, a missing title, or a salvage, rebuilt or flood brand changes what the car is worth and whether you can insure it.

  3. Warning Lamps That Stay On, Or Never Come On

    Every lamp should light when the key turns on, then go out once the engine runs. A lamp that never lights may have had its bulb pulled or its cluster altered.

  4. Readiness Monitors Not Ready

    A cheap OBD-II scanner shows whether the car has finished its self tests. Several monitors showing not ready means someone cleared the codes or pulled the battery just before you arrived.

  5. Frame Rust Or Frame Repair

    Surface rust on bolts is normal. Flaking metal, holes, or fresh welds on the frame rails are not. Fresh black undercoating on an old truck deserves a hard look.

  6. Milky Oil Or Oily Coolant

    Oil that looks like chocolate milk, or coolant with an oily film, points to coolant and oil mixing inside the engine. That is one of the costliest repairs a car can need.

  7. Damp Carpet And A Musty Smell

    Lift the floor mats and the trunk floor. Water lines, silt under the seats, rusty seat bolts and a heavy air freshener are the classic signs of a flood car.

  8. Paint And Gaps That Do Not Match

    One panel a shade off, overspray on rubber seals, or a gap wider on one side than the other all mean body work. Ask what was hit and who fixed it.

  9. The Engine Is Already Warm

    A cold engine shows its faults. Hard starts, lifter tick, smoke and rough idle often fade once the engine warms up. Ask the seller not to start it before you get there.

  10. Pressure To Decide Today

    Another buyer on the way, cash only, a meeting spot far from the seller home. A good car at a fair price does not need a deadline.

What To Do When You Find One

One red flag is a reason to slow down. Two is a reason to leave. If you still want the car, put the finding in front of a professional before any money moves. Our guide on when to hire an inspector covers how that works.
