Paperwork And VIN Checks

A clean car with a bad title is still a bad buy. Check the paper as hard as the paint.

By Anthony Limpert, mobile mechanic and shop owner. Author source EEAT Authors.

Three Free VIN Lookups

The VIN is the 17 character number on the driver side of the dash and on the door jamb sticker. Ask for it before you drive anywhere.

  • Open Safety Recalls

    The NHTSA recall lookup shows safety recalls on that exact car that were never repaired. Recall repairs are free at a dealer for that brand.

  • Theft And Salvage Records

    NICB VINCheck shows whether a member insurance company has reported the car stolen and not recovered, or as a salvage vehicle. It allows five searches in 24 hours.

  • Title History

    The federal NMVTIS vehicle history site lists approved report providers. A report shows title brands such as salvage, junk and flood, plus the last odometer reading on record. Providers charge a small fee.

The Buyers Guide Sticker

Under the federal Used Car Rule, a dealer that sells more than five used vehicles in a year must post a Buyers Guide on every used car it offers. The FTC guide to buying a used car from a dealer explains each box.

The sticker says whether the car comes with a warranty or is sold as is. As is means you pay for every repair after you drive away. Spoken promises are hard to enforce, so have the dealer write them on the Buyers Guide, and keep your copy. Private sellers usually do not have to post one.

The Title

Read the front and the back. The seller name should match their ID. Look for a lien holder, and for brands such as salvage, rebuilt, flood or lemon law buyback. The VIN on the title must match the car in two places, the dash and the door jamb.

The Bill Of Sale

Write one even when your state does not require it. Include the date, the price, the VIN, the mileage, and the printed names and signatures of both people. Add any promise the seller made, such as a second key or a set of winter tires.

State Checks

Many states require an emissions test or a safety inspection before a car can be registered. Ask your state motor vehicle office who must provide it, and do not accept a promise that it will pass.
