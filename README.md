# Used Car Inspection Checklist

A free 69 point checklist for anyone buying a used car. The live version saves your ticks as you go.

Live site https://clickflame.github.io/used-car-inspection-checklist/


## Before You Go

Ten minutes at home saves a wasted drive.

- [ ] Ask the seller for the VIN before you leave
- [ ] Search the VIN at NHTSA for open safety recalls
- [ ] Run the VIN through NICB VINCheck for theft and salvage records
- [ ] Pull a title history report from an approved NMVTIS provider
- [ ] Ask for service records and count the oil changes
- [ ] Ask how long they have owned it and why they are selling
- [ ] Look up what the same year and mileage sells for near you
- [ ] Plan to see it in daylight, dry, with a cold engine

## Walk Around

Start ten feet back, then move in close.

- [ ] Sight down each side for waves and dents in the body lines
- [ ] Check the panel gaps are even left to right
- [ ] Look for paint that does not match from panel to panel
- [ ] Look for paint overspray on rubber seals and trim
- [ ] Check every piece of glass for chips and cracks
- [ ] Open and close every door, the hood and the trunk
- [ ] Check each lamp for haze, cracks and water inside
- [ ] Look for rust at the wheel arches, rockers and door bottoms
- [ ] Push down hard on each corner, it should settle in one bounce
- [ ] Match the VIN on the dash to the door jamb sticker and the title

## Tires And Brakes

Tires tell you how the car was driven and aligned.

- [ ] Measure tread on all four tires, 2/32 inch is worn out
- [ ] Budget for a new set if any tire is at 4/32 inch or less
- [ ] Check wear is even across each tire, edge wear points to alignment
- [ ] Read the four digit date code on the sidewall, week then year
- [ ] Confirm all four tires are the same size and a matched set
- [ ] Check each wheel for curb damage and bends
- [ ] Look through the wheel at the rotor for deep grooves or a raised lip
- [ ] Look behind each wheel for wet shocks and torn rubber boots
- [ ] Confirm the spare, jack and lug wrench are in the car

## Under The Hood

Do this before the engine is started.

- [ ] Touch the hood, a warm engine can hide a hard cold start
- [ ] Pull the dipstick, oil should sit between the marks and not look milky
- [ ] Check the coolant tank is at the line and not rusty or oily
- [ ] Check the brake fluid is at the line and not dark brown
- [ ] Look for wet spots and fresh oil on the engine and hoses
- [ ] Check the belts for cracks and frayed edges
- [ ] Check the battery posts for crusty buildup and find the date sticker
- [ ] Look at the fender and radiator support bolts for wrench marks
- [ ] Look for new parts next to old ones and ask what was replaced

## Inside

Press every button. Yes, every one.

- [ ] Turn the key on, every warning lamp should light, then go out after start
- [ ] Compare the odometer to the service records
- [ ] Check pedal, seat and wheel wear fits the mileage
- [ ] Smell for mildew, then lift the mats and feel the carpet for damp
- [ ] Lift the trunk floor and check the spare well for water and rust
- [ ] Run the air cold and the heat hot at every fan speed
- [ ] Work every window, lock, mirror and seat control
- [ ] Buckle and pull every seat belt
- [ ] Test the screen, backup camera, speakers and charge ports

## Underneath

Bring a flashlight and something to kneel on.

- [ ] Look at the ground where it was parked for fresh drips
- [ ] Check the frame rails for flaking rust and holes
- [ ] Look for bent, cut or welded frame sections
- [ ] Check the exhaust for rust holes and broken hangers
- [ ] Check the axle boots for splits and flung grease
- [ ] Be wary of fresh undercoating on an older car

## Road Test

Twenty minutes, radio off, windows up then down.

- [ ] Start it yourself, it should crank fast and idle smooth
- [ ] Watch the tailpipe at start for blue or white smoke
- [ ] Listen for ticking or knocking in the first minute
- [ ] Shift into drive and reverse, each should engage right away
- [ ] Feel every shift, none should slip, flare or bang
- [ ] On a flat straight road the car should track straight
- [ ] Brake firmly from 40 mph, no pull, no pulsing pedal, no grinding
- [ ] Reach highway speed and feel for shake in the wheel or seat
- [ ] Drive over bumps and listen for clunks
- [ ] Turn tight slow circles both ways, clicking means a worn axle joint
- [ ] Park, leave it running, check the temperature gauge and look under for drips

## Before You Pay

The last five minutes matter the most.

- [ ] Plug in an OBD-II scanner and read any stored codes
- [ ] Check the readiness monitors, several not ready means codes were cleared
- [ ] Read the Buyers Guide sticker on a dealer car, As Is means you pay for repairs
- [ ] Read the title for liens and for salvage, rebuilt or flood brands
- [ ] Confirm the name on the title matches the seller ID
- [ ] Get every promise in writing on the bill of sale
- [ ] Get a bill of sale with the VIN, price, date and both signatures
